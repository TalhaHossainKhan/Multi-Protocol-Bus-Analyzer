================================================================================
  DAQ PLATFORM — SLACK & MICROSOFT TEAMS ALARM NOTIFICATIONS
  Setup Guide
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
--------
When an alarm fires during a live recording session, the DAQ Platform can send
an instant notification to a Slack or Microsoft Teams channel of your choice.

There is NO login, NO account linking, and NO third-party SDK required.
It works through a standard "Incoming Webhook" URL — a one-time setup that
takes about 2 minutes per channel.  Once pasted into Settings, it works
automatically forever.

The software uses only Python's built-in urllib library.  No data leaves your
machine except for the alarm notification itself (alarm name, channel name,
triggered value, device name).  Raw sensor data is NEVER sent.


================================================================================
  SLACK SETUP
================================================================================

Step 1 — Create an Incoming Webhook for your workspace
-------------------------------------------------------
1. Go to:  https://api.slack.com/apps
2. Click "Create New App" → choose "From scratch"
3. Give it a name (e.g. "DAQ Alarms") and pick your workspace → Create App
4. In the left sidebar click "Incoming Webhooks"
5. Toggle "Activate Incoming Webhooks" to ON
6. Scroll down and click "Add New Webhook to Workspace"
7. Pick the channel you want notifications to go to (e.g. #daq-alerts)
8. Click "Allow"
9. Copy the Webhook URL — it looks like:
      https://hooks.slack.com/services/<TEAM_ID>/<WEBHOOK_ID>/<SECRET_TOKEN>
   (three slash-separated IDs after /services/ — keep it private, it grants
    posting rights to your channel)

Step 2 — Paste into the DAQ Platform
--------------------------------------
1. Open the DAQ Platform and connect your device
2. Go to Settings tab → Alarms section
3. Scroll to the "Notifications" subsection at the bottom
4. Paste the webhook URL into the "Webhook URL" field
5. Click "Send Test Message" — check your Slack channel for a green tick message
6. Done.  Every alarm that fires will now post to that channel.

What a notification looks like in Slack:
-----------------------------------------
  🚨  Alarm fired: OverheatWarning
  Channel `temperature` = *87.3*  ·  device: *ESP32_MotorTest*


================================================================================
  MICROSOFT TEAMS SETUP
================================================================================

Step 1 — Add an Incoming Webhook connector to a Teams channel
--------------------------------------------------------------
1. Open Microsoft Teams and go to the channel you want (e.g. "DAQ Alerts")
2. Click the three-dot menu (···) next to the channel name → "Connectors"
   (If you don't see Connectors: click "Manage channel" → "Connectors")
3. Search for "Incoming Webhook" → click "Add" → "Add" again
4. Give it a name (e.g. "DAQ Platform") and optionally upload an icon
5. Click "Create"
6. Copy the Webhook URL — it looks like:
      https://yourorg.webhook.office.com/webhookb2/XXXXXXXX@XXXXXXXX/
      IncomingWebhook/XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX/XXXXXXXX-XXXX-...

Step 2 — Paste into the DAQ Platform
--------------------------------------
1. Open the DAQ Platform and connect your device
2. Go to Settings tab → Alarms section
3. Scroll to the "Notifications" subsection at the bottom
4. Paste the webhook URL into the "Webhook URL" field
5. Click "Send Test Message" — check your Teams channel for a confirmation message
6. Done.

NOTE: Teams renders the plain "text" field from the payload.  The alarm name,
channel name, value, and device name will all appear in the message.
Block-kit formatting (Slack-specific) is silently ignored by Teams.


================================================================================
  USING BOTH SLACK AND TEAMS
================================================================================

The DAQ Platform currently supports one webhook URL per session.  If you need
to notify both Slack and Teams simultaneously:

Option A — Slack workflow automation:
  Set up a Slack workflow that mirrors messages from #daq-alerts to a Teams
  channel via the Teams connector.

Option B — Teams Power Automate:
  Create a Power Automate flow triggered by the Teams incoming webhook that
  also posts to a Slack channel.

Option C — Use a webhook relay service (e.g. Zapier, Make):
  Point the DAQ Platform at your Zapier/Make webhook URL; configure it to
  fan out to both Slack and Teams.  Zapier offers a free tier that is
  sufficient for alarm volumes typical in bench testing.


================================================================================
  TROUBLESHOOTING
================================================================================

No message received after "Send Test Message":
  • Check the URL is pasted correctly (no trailing space, full URL).
  • Make sure the Slack app is still installed in your workspace
    (api.slack.com/apps → verify the app exists and is active).
  • For Teams: verify the connector is still active on the channel
    (channel → Connectors → Configured → check Incoming Webhook is listed).
  • Firewall: outbound HTTPS (port 443) to hooks.slack.com or
    *.webhook.office.com must be allowed on your network.

Message arrives but looks garbled:
  • Teams: only the "text" field is rendered — this is expected.
  • Slack: make sure you chose "Incoming Webhooks" and not "Event Subscriptions".

Alarm fires during recording but no message arrives:
  • Confirm the Webhook URL field is still populated (Settings → Alarms).
    The URL is saved across sessions via system preferences, but verify.
  • The software silently swallows network errors to avoid interrupting your
    test.  If the network goes down mid-test, notifications are dropped
    (the recording itself is unaffected).

URL expired or rotated:
  • Slack and Teams webhook URLs do not expire unless manually revoked or
    the app is uninstalled.  If you rotate it, just paste the new URL.


================================================================================
  PRIVACY & PROPRIETARY DATA
================================================================================

Only the following information is sent in a webhook notification:
  - Alarm name (defined by you in the Settings panel)
  - Channel name (defined by your firmware)
  - The value that triggered the alarm (a single float)
  - Device name (the label you see in the top bar)
  - A timestamp

Raw sensor waveforms, CSV data, session configs, and any other session content
are NEVER sent.  The webhook call is a one-way fire-and-forget POST of ~200
bytes of JSON per alarm event.

If your company policy prohibits any data leaving the network, you can:
  a) Leave the Webhook URL field blank (alarms still log locally, no POST made).
  b) Use an internal webhook relay on your own infrastructure that accepts the
     same JSON format and routes to an internal alerting system.
